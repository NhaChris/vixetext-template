# Abstract

Learning programming logic is among the main difficulties faced by students entering computing programs, and introductory algorithm courses account for a large share of failures and dropout in the field. Gamification initiatives designed to address this problem, however, are often limited to external rewards such as points, badges and leaderboards, without changing the nature of the programming activity itself. This research project presents CodeMage, a turn-based combat game in which building algorithms is the core mechanic: instead of selecting ready-made abilities, the player programs the spells of a character in a restricted subset of the Lua language, written in an editor embedded in the game and executed in an isolated environment (*sandbox*). Making progress requires decomposing each problem, understanding why a solution failed, debugging it and generalizing it, since opponents have immunities that force the player to rethink their strategies. The research is applied in nature, exploratory and descriptive, and adopts Design Science Research as its method. In this first stage, the work comprised a literature review on computational thinking, gamification and Problem-Based Learning, the analysis of related games and tools, and the construction of a working prototype whose spell language was formalized as a context-free grammar. The prototype indicates that the code written by the learner can be the center of the game experience without compromising the security or stability of the system. The next stage will evaluate the game with students of introductory programming courses in order to verify its effects on the participants' algorithmic reasoning.
